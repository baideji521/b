=====================

There is ONE input file: the MERGED ANALYSIS FILE.

It contains FOUR sections, in this order:

TIMESTAMP FORMAT — READ THIS BEFORE ANYTHING ELSE:
Every `[start - end]` in every section is ABSOLUTE SECONDS FROM THE START OF THE VIDEO,
written as a decimal number. `[124.27 - 134.81]` means 124.27s to 134.81s. It is NOT
mm:ss. Do not strip, merge, or reinterpret any part of the number, and do not multiply
minutes by 60 — there are no minutes in this file.
Every timestamp lies between 0 and the `Duration` value in the file header.
If a number you are about to output is larger than that `Duration`, you misread a
timestamp: go back to SECTION 4 and copy the value again, character for character.


SECTION 1 — INTERLEAVED TIMELINE
One line per item, sorted by time:
* `[start - end] Visual (emotion score) [importance]: description`
  followed by an indented `On-screen text: ...` line when the frame has text
* `[start - end] Speech (speaker N) (emotion score): what is said`

SECTION 2 — ACTION TRACK
`[start - end]: action_label @ scene_label`
One entry per action span. This is WHAT IS PHYSICALLY HAPPENING and WHEN IT CHANGES.

SECTION 3 — EXPRESSION TRACK
`[start - end]: expression intensity`
Facial expression from a dedicated face model, sampled at 2 fps, 0.5s resolution.
This is the PRIMARY emotional evidence.
It describes ONLY the largest face on screen. When two people are visible, it is one
person's expression — do not attribute it to whoever happens to be speaking.
A line like `--- 2.00s with no face detected (this is NOT neutral) ---` marks a hole in
the track. A hole means no measurement, not a calm face and not an emotion ending.
Never read neutral into it and never fill it from the Visual line's emotion.

SECTION 4 — WORD-BY-WORD TIMELINE
`[start - end] word`
One timestamp per word. THIS SECTION IS THE FINAL TIMESTAMP AUTHORITY.
A line like `--- 8.56s with no speech ---` marks silence between two words. It means
NOBODY IS TALKING there — it does NOT mean nothing is happening. Judge such a stretch on
Sections 2 and 3: an action change or an expression transition inside it makes it a real
beat (a reaction, a look, a physical gag) and the clip MAY span it. Only when the action
track and the expression track are both flat across the stretch is it dead air to be
trimmed. Never treat the words on either side of a marker as one continuous sentence.

There is NO separate second file. Wherever this prompt refers to "the word-level
timestamp file", it means SECTION 4 of this same file.

Sections 1-3 tell you WHAT HAPPENS and WHICH MOMENT IS EDITORIALLY VALUABLE.
Section 4 tells you WHEN THE WORD ACTUALLY STARTS and ENDS.

2A. EVIDENCE HIERARCHY
======================

A highlight is defined by ACTION + FACIAL EXPRESSION + SPEECH.

TIER 1 — DECISIVE. A highlight MUST be supported by these:
* Action track (Section 2) — the action and the moment it changes
* Expression track (Section 3) — facial expression, 0.5s resolution
* Word-level speech (Section 4) — what is said and exactly when

TIER 1.5 — SUPPORTING, OFTEN CARRIES THE HOOK, BUT NOT TRUSTWORTHY AS TEXT:
* On-screen text (Section 1) — read by an OCR pass and frequently misrecognised.
  Observed in real output: "twister2" for a candy wrapper, "fresh eggs" for a piece of
  chocolate. Use it only when it agrees with the Visual description of the same span.
  NEVER quote it as fact, never build a premise on it alone, and never put it in the
  output text.

TIER 2 — REFERENCE ONLY. May confirm or break a tie, never create a candidate:
* Audio emotion — the `(emotion score)` on Speech lines. One label per whole
  sentence (often 5-10s), too coarse to locate a moment, frequently wrong at high
  confidence, and sometimes `<unk>`.
* The `(emotion score)` on Visual lines when no expression track entry overlaps.

RULES:
1. NEVER select a candidate on audio emotion alone.
2. Audio emotion may move a score by at most a few points.
3. If audio emotion contradicts the expression track, follow the expression track
   and ignore the audio emotion for that moment.
4. A missing or `<unk>` audio emotion is NOT a penalty.
5. `importance` on Visual lines is a precomputed label (fall / collision / drop /
   sudden entry / drastic change -> high or critical). Every `high` and `critical`
   span enters Stage 1 discovery automatically.

==================================================
2B. THE STRONGEST PATTERN: TRIPLE SYNCHRONIZATION
=================================================

The highest-scoring moment is where all three land within the same ~2 seconds:

1. an action-track label change (e.g. eating -> hand_gesture)
2. an expression-track transition (e.g. happy -> surprised, or happy -> sad)
3. a spoken line that names or reacts to what just happened

A speaker switch inside that window makes it stronger still: one speaker sets up,
the other reacts.

All three coincide -> this is the peak; score it highest.
Two coincide -> strong candidate.
One only -> weak; do not select unless nothing better exists.

Audio emotion is NOT one of the three. It only confirms.

SPEAKER BOUNDARIES:

Speech lines carry `(speaker N)`. Do not start or end the clip in the middle of one
speaker's turn unless the selected word range is self-contained. A setup line from one
speaker plus the other speaker's reply is a complete unit - keep both or neither.

Speaker labels come from a voiceprint model and are assigned per line, so they can be
wrong on short lines. A speaker change only counts as a real exchange when the words
themselves read as one person answering another. If the two lines read as one continuous
sentence split in half, treat it as ONE turn and do not score it as an exchange.

ON-SCREEN TEXT:

A segment whose on-screen text states the premise scores higher on standalone clarity
than one that depends on earlier context — but only if that text agrees with the Visual
description of the same span. On-screen text comes from OCR and is often wrong; when it
disagrees with the Visual line, ignore it rather than inventing a premise from it.

2C. ADAPTIVE CLIP BOUNDARIES
============================

Decide the START by asking ONE question: does the payoff make sense on its own?

CASE A — CONTEXT-DEPENDENT (a rule, bet, promise or expectation was spoken first):
Set clip.start at the FIRST WORD of the premise sentence, not at the reaction.
Adding the premise is REQUIRED whenever total duration stays <= 15.00.
Real example from this data set:
  premise   53.82  "If this is yellow, I get to go shopping today."
  payoff 1  57.67  "No way, it was yellow."
  payoff 2  63.71  "Green."          (a second, bigger payoff)
  correct clip: 53.82 - 63.71 = 9.89s
Starting at 57.67 instead is WRONG: the viewer never learns why shopping is at
stake. A clip that is shorter but unintelligible is a defect, not a tighter edit.
Cutting the premise to save seconds is only allowed when the 15.00 cap forces it.

CASE B — SELF-EXPLANATORY (a fall, a reveal, a physical gag, a pure reaction, with no
spoken prerequisite):
Set clip.start 1.0-2.0s before the trigger, on the nearest real boundary: a word start
in SECTION 4, or an action/expression-track boundary in SECTION 2/3.

IF PREMISE + PAYOFF > 15.00:
Drop the premise, keep action + peak reaction, and carry the rule in the `comment`
overlay instead (for the example above: "Yellow = shopping spree").

END BOUNDARY:
* Never end mid-word or mid-sentence. End at the END of the last word you keep.
* A silent tail is allowed only if the expression track still has an entry there
  (something is still readable on the face) and only up to 1.0s. Otherwise end exactly
  at the last word.
* clip.end never exceeds the `Duration` in the file header.

NO ARITHMETIC PADDING:
Do NOT apply -0.3s / +0.5s style padding to make the cut "safer". Every number you
output must be a value that appears in SECTION 4 (or in SECTION 2/3 for a Case-B start).
Invented values are the single most common failure mode of this task.

TWO PAYOFFS IN ONE WINDOW:
If two payoffs land within 15.00s of each other, keep BOTH in one continuous clip — a
double payoff outscores either half alone. Pick the highest-scoring candidate in the
video, not the earliest one.

IN THE `reason` FIELD:
State explicitly whether the premise is inside the clip. If it is not, say which overlay
carries the rule instead.

28. SAME TIME + DIFFERENT EMOTION
=================================

If the expression track and the audio emotion overlap but conflict:

FOLLOW THE EXPRESSION TRACK. Do NOT compare their confidence values.

The two numbers come from different models on different signals and are NOT on a
comparable scale. A high audio score does not mean the audio label is right.

Example:

Expression track:

surprised 0.62

Audio:

happy 0.94

PRIMARY EMOTION:

surprised

because the expression track decides, regardless of 0.94 > 0.62.

Example:

Expression track:

happy 0.96

Audio:

surprised 0.71

PRIMARY EMOTION:

happy

Real observed failures in this data: "Pink." scored angry 1.00, "Yeah, it's green"
scored sad 0.99, while the face model showed happy at the same moments. Following the
higher number would have been wrong both times.

29. THE EXPRESSION TRACK DECIDES
================================

When the expression track and the audio emotion conflict during the same interval:

PRIMARY_EMOTION = the expression-track label (Section 3), always.

The audio emotion may:

* confirm the expression track when they agree
* break a tie between two candidates that are otherwise equal

The audio emotion may NOT:

* override the expression track
* create a candidate on its own
* move a score by more than a few points

Do NOT average conflicting emotions.

Do NOT rank the two modalities by confidence.

Confidence values are comparable ONLY within the same modality:
